# L1 Capstone — Spot & Annotate AI Behavior

**Goal:** demonstrate you can tell confident-correct from confident-guessing AI without
checking every claim — the L1 "move on when" bar. This capstone produces a **real artifact**:
an annotated transcript that shows your judgment on the page.

## Part 1 — Annotate a transcript (the deliverable)

Take an agent transcript — one provided to you, or a real session of your own — and mark it up
turn by turn:

- Flag each AI turn as **good behavior** (grounded, admits uncertainty, asks before acting) or
  **bad behavior** (hallucinates, over-claims, acts on a false premise, gold-plates).
- For each flag, write one line: *what tipped you off.*
- Call out at least one moment the AI was **confidently wrong** and one where it was
  **correctly uncertain**.

The annotated transcript is what you hand in.

## Part 2 — AI-claims practical exam

1. Pick a codebase or domain you know well.
2. Ask AI ~10 questions where you already know the answers — mix easy, obscure, and
   "edge of its knowledge", and slip in 2–3 questions with false premises.
3. For each answer, **before** verifying, predict: correct, or confidently guessing? Note the
   signal you used.
4. Verify each answer and score your predictions.

## Passing bar

- The annotated transcript shows specific, defensible judgments — not just "this looks fine".
- You correctly flagged the hallucinations and the false-premise questions in the exam.
- You can articulate *what tipped you off* (vagueness, over-specific detail on rare facts,
  refusing to say "I don't know") — not just that you got lucky.
- You map at least one failure to one of the four properties (Next Token Prediction,
  Knowledge, Working Memory, Steerability).

Attach the annotated transcript and your scored predictions as evidence.
