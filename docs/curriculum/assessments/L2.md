# L2 Capstone — Bring an AI Change to Merge Quality

**Goal:** show that reviewing diffs and pushing for simplification is now a *habit* that
produces mergeable work — the L2 "move on when" bar. This capstone produces a **real
deliverable**: a merged (or merge-ready) change you took from raw AI output to quality.

## Part 1 — The build (the deliverable)

Take an AI-generated multi-file change — one you prompt for now, or a draft you already have —
and bring it to merge quality yourself:

- **Review the diff line by line.** Find and fix the plausible-but-wrong parts (the subtle
  bug, the wrong assumption, the case the AI didn't consider).
- **Write the missing tests.** AI-written tests usually only cover what the AI built — add the
  cases it missed, especially boundaries and error paths.
- **Simplify.** Cut at least one instance of overcomplicated or gold-plated code and confirm
  the result is better.
- **Keep it surgical** — every line traces to the intent; prune drive-by edits.

The reviewed, tested, simplified change (with its PR) is what you hand in. See the annotated
[golden example — a good AI-assisted PR](../examples/ai-assisted-pr.md) for the bar.

## Part 2 — SPACE reflection (the evidence)

Alongside the build, keep a short log and audit it against the **SPACE** framework — a balanced
view of productivity, not just output volume:

- **S**atisfaction & well-being · **P**erformance (quality) · **A**ctivity (volume) ·
  **C**ommunication & collaboration · **E**fficiency & flow

## Passing bar

- The PR is genuinely merge-ready: the plausible-but-wrong parts are caught and fixed, and
  the tests are yours (they cover what the AI's tests missed).
- You can point to at least one thing you **caught in review** and one place you **simplified**.
- The diff is surgical — every line traces to the intent.
- A short SPACE reflection: did AI improve *outcomes and flow*, or just raise activity volume?
  Honest "where it slowed me down" notes count in your favour.

Attach the PR (with review trail) and your SPACE reflection as evidence.
